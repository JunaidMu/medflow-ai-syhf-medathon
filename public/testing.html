Requirements are derived from the prototype’s own disclosed, in-source rule set and code comments. They are software requirements, not clinical requirements.

Known limitations

  1. Rule coverage is small and disclosed on purpose. Many real symptoms and phrasings in both languages are unrecognized and fall back to “unclassified”, which depends entirely on human triage.
  2. Negation support is narrow. Only exact listed phrases are stripped: no / denies / without (English) and لا اعاني من / لا يوجد / بدون (Arabic). Confirmed gaps: first-person “deny” (B2), negation scope across “or” (B4), Arabic negators with intervening words (C6), colloquial negators such as ما في (D3).
  3. English matching is word-order dependent. “chest pain” matches; “pain in my chest” does not (A6). “trouble breathing” is unrecognized (A2).
  4. Arabic orthography variants are not unified. The disclosed fever term حراره (haa) does not match MSA حرارة (taa marbuta) — C5.
  5. Colloquial Arabic wording may be unrecognized. Colloquial cardiac pain (وجع قلبي … صدري) falls back to “unclassified” (D1).
  6. Vital-sign input ranges are broad. Physiologically implausible but in-range values (e.g., SpO₂ 45) are accepted; range plausibility requires clinical review.
  7. Speech recognition is not connected. Transcripts are typed or manually confirmed by the patient.
  8. No clinical validation of any kind. No sensitivity, specificity, diagnostic accuracy, or patient-safety claims can be made from these results. The prototype performs no ESI assignment, diagnosis, or treatment.
  9. Demo data handling. Browser localStorage is unencrypted and the demo clinician role is not real authentication. The prototype is not connected to any hospital system.
  10. The test suite itself is software-level. All 30 scenarios are fictional; there is no clinical ground truth, and every case is labeled “Not clinically reviewed”.
